Post-mortem, not a product. ReserveMyTokens was stopped before launch: there is no network, no workers and no users, and credits never had cash value. The ledger rows and receipts below were exported by running the real coordinator code on a throwaway database with a scripted worker that replays one real Ollama job. ReserveMyTokens: the ledger worked, the workers could lie

A lab project from October 2026: volunteers run a local open-weight model, a coordinator sends them prompts, and they earn test credits per completion token. The accounting held up under every race we threw at it. The project still had to stop, because a worker is paid on a number it reports itself, and nobody had asked for the capacity in the first place.

1. One job, as double-entry postings

Job: "What is the capital of France? Answer in one word.", max_tokens 64, model qwen2.5:0.5b. On a real Ollama 0.40.6 run on 2026-10-21 it answered "Paris" with 41 prompt and 2 completion tokens. The consumer starts with 1000 test credits.

2. Check the worker's signed receipt yourself

Each worker signs job | worker | sha256(output) | prompt tokens | completion tokens | duration with its own Ed25519 key. Your browser checks the signature below with WebCrypto. Nothing is sent anywhere. Change a number and check again.

The catch: switch to the lying worker in section 1 and verify its receipt. It is valid. A signature proves which worker made a claim, not that the claim is true. The lying worker returned an empty answer, signed a claim of 64 tokens, and the coordinator paid it 64, because the charge is min(reported tokens, max_tokens). Spot checks (re-running a sample on a second worker, known-answer probes) only catch some of it, and two workers run by one person agree with each other.

3. What the tests proved

  • Every ledger transaction sums to zero in integer units, and each write has a fixed idempotency key, so a retry is a no-op.
  • Two workers racing for one job: exactly one gets it. Six identical results fired at once: one settles, five are no-ops. A late result racing the lease timeout either settles or refunds, never both.
  • The whole backend suite passed on Postgres 15 (38 tests) and on SQLite (36, plus 2 Postgres-only skips), in normal and reversed file order. Removing the compare-and-set on results makes the race test fail.

5. Why it stopped

  1. Terms came after the pitch. The first page offered to pool people's AI subscriptions. The provider terms, read three days later, bar sharing an account or handing over API keys. The only clean supply left was local open-weight models, which are cheap to get elsewhere.
  2. No one was asked. No consumer or donor interview happened before or during the build.
  3. Payouts rest on what the worker says (section 2).
  4. Prior art found late (section 4).
